# Mioitalia editorial style guide (IMAT-level originals) — FROZEN TEMPLATE v1

Distilled from studying the official IMAT papers, weighting **2023–2025** most heavily. The gate for
every question: *"Would an experienced IMAT candidate believe this came from a real future IMAT paper?"*
If not, rewrite it.

> **This template is frozen (v1, approved 2026-07-08).** Use it unchanged for every batch. Changing
> the difficulty targets, schema, or style below requires an explicit new approved version.

## 0. Non-negotiable editorial mandates

1. **Harder than recent official.** The average item must sit ~15–20% above the difficulty of recent
   official IMAT papers. See the difficulty targets in §2 (deliberately shifted up).
2. **2023–2025 first.** Prioritise the concepts, wording, and reasoning patterns of 2023–2025 papers.
   Treat 2011–2016 styles as historical; do not imitate their occasional simple-recall items.
3. **No isolated recall.** Avoid single-fact definition questions. Every item should require multi-step
   reasoning, data/scenario interpretation, or integration of ≥2 concepts. A pure "what is X?" is only
   acceptable if reframed into an applied/inferential context.
4. **Distractors are misconceptions.** Every wrong option must be scientifically plausible and encode a
   specific, common candidate error — never filler.
5. **Original but indistinguishable.** Each question must be completely original yet feel like it could
   appear verbatim on a real future IMAT paper.

## 2. Difficulty (target mix — shifted UP; average clearly above recent official papers)

- **Medium ~15%** — one core concept but still applied (a reasoning step or a small calculation),
  never a bare definition. This is the floor, not the norm.
- **Hard ~50%** — multi-step, integrates 2 concepts, or a calculation with a genuine trap. The backbone.
- **Very hard ~35%** — deep integration, multi-step quantitative reasoning, or subtle data interpretation
  with distractors that encode real misconceptions. A substantial minority of every batch.

Do **not** author easy or pure-recall questions. Per §0, average difficulty is deliberately ~15–20%
above recent official IMAT. When unsure between two tiers, choose the harder framing.

## 3. Topic emphasis (recent-trend weighted)

- **Biology:** biochemistry & metabolism (glycolysis, Krebs, oxidative phosphorylation/chemiosmosis),
  molecular biology (transcription/translation, mutations, gene regulation), cell biology & membranes,
  genetics (probability, linkage), human physiology (nervous, cardiovascular, renal, endocrine, muscle),
  immunology basics. Recent papers lean heavily biochemical and molecular — weight those.
- **Chemistry:** stoichiometry & yield, gas laws, equilibrium (Kc/Kp, Le Chatelier, dissociation),
  acids/bases & buffers & titration, redox & oxidation numbers, atomic structure & periodic trends,
  bonding & molecular shape/polarity, organic functional groups & isomerism, solutions & colligative
  properties.
- **Physics:** kinematics & dynamics, energy/work/power, momentum & impulse, circular motion & gravitation,
  fluids & pressure, thermodynamics & gas processes, electricity (circuits, fields), electromagnetism,
  waves/optics. Favour multi-step numeric problems with clean-ish answers.
- **Mathematics:** algebra & inequalities, functions (incl. logs/exponentials), equations (incl. absolute
  value, quadratics), probability & combinatorics, sequences, plane geometry & trigonometry, analytic
  geometry. IMAT math is problem-solving, not proof.
- **Critical Thinking & Reading:** argument analysis (main conclusion, assumption, flaw, weaken/strengthen,
  parallel reasoning), data/quantitative reasoning, short logic puzzles. Reading = one short passage +
  an inference/interpretation question. Keep general-knowledge trivia minimal (it is being de-emphasised).

## 4. Writing style rules

- **Length:** stems typically 1–4 sentences (reading/CT up to a short paragraph). Give exactly the data
  needed; no padding, no unstated assumptions.
- **Options:** always **five (A–E)**, mutually exclusive, similar length/grammar, plausible. The correct
  answer must be unambiguously correct; each distractor should be *wrong for a reason* (a specific
  misconception or a common calculation slip), never a throwaway.
- **Numbers:** choose values that keep arithmetic clean where possible; provide constants the candidate
  needs (g, Ka, γ, log values). Units stated and consistent.
- **Register:** neutral, precise, exam-formal British/International English (as official IMAT). No humour,
  no cultural specificity, no ambiguity.
- **Science:** current and correct. No outdated terminology, no oversimplifications that are technically
  wrong. Every answer must be scientifically defensible on its own.
- **Integration:** prefer questions that connect two ideas (e.g. stoichiometry + gas law; genetics +
  probability; ETC + thermodynamics) — this is the hallmark of hard IMAT items.

## 5. Distractor design

- Encode the most common student errors: sign errors, forgetting a factor, confusing competitive vs
  non-competitive inhibition, correlation-vs-causation, using speeds instead of velocities, wrong
  exponent in a power law, complement-rule slips, off-by-one in stoichiometry.
- Avoid "none/all of the above" unless it is genuinely the best original design.
- No two distractors should be equivalent; exactly one option is correct.

## 6. Originality checklist (per question)

- [ ] Not a paraphrase/number-swap of any official item; tests a new situation.
- [ ] Not a near-duplicate of an already-accepted Mioitalia item.
- [ ] No reused official figure; self-contained in text (or an original figure supplied).
- [ ] Scientifically accurate; correct answer defensible; all distractors plausible & wrong-for-a-reason.
- [ ] Difficulty tagged honestly; sits at or above the recent-official level.
- [ ] Explanation teaches the concept and explains every wrong option.
- [ ] Passes the gate: *plausibly a real future IMAT question.*

## 7. Production loop

Author in editorial batches of ~100 (roughly per-subject proportional to the composition table), each:
**generate → adversarial self-review (science, distractors, originality, difficulty) → correct/reject →
commit accepted items**. Quality over volume; a rejected question is cheaper than a wrong one shipped.
